\author{}
\date{Revised July 30, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We study a statistical consequence of a stratified-manifold hypothesis for
vision representations. The global hypothesis says that each regular token
$t$ has a neighborhood in the representation support that is homeomorphic to
an intrinsic Euclidean space. Topology alone does not specify distances or
sampling probabilities, so it cannot directly determine a finite-sample test.
We therefore introduce a stronger localized null: conditional on a sufficiently
small ball around $t$, representation mass is uniform with respect to intrinsic
volume. Under this null, mass in a radius-$r$ ball grows as $r^d$, and the
probability of a concentric shell is the difference of two normalized
$d$-dimensional volumes. We estimate the local dimension, transform the radii
into fitted equal-probability shells, and compare observed shell counts with the
uniform shell PMF using the likelihood-ratio statistic
$T_N=2N\KL(\widehat Q\|P)$. Because dimension and shells are fitted from the
same neighborhood, we calibrate the complete procedure by Monte Carlo
simulation. Synthetic experiments recover the nominal size and gain power
against center- and boundary-heavy alternatives. On ImageNet patches, LlamaGen
VQ codes, and VAR VQ codes, rejection rates are $33.04\%$, $86.83\%$, and
$13.28\%$, respectively. These results reject a universal locally homogeneous
volume model, but they do not by themselves prove stratification: density,
curvature, anisotropy, boundaries, and quantization can produce the same radial
signature.
\end{abstract}

\section{From a Topological Claim to a Statistical Null}

Let $\mathcal E\subseteq\R^{D_{\mathrm{amb}}}$ be the support of a vision
representation and let $t\in\mathcal E$ be an anchor token. The global
local-Euclidean hypothesis can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_{\mathrm{top}}:\qquad
  \forall t\in\mathcal E,\quad
  \exists\ r_\ast(t)>0,\ d(t)\le D_{\mathrm{amb}}
  \quad\text{such that}\quad
  \mathcal E\cap \Ball^\circ_{D_{\mathrm{amb}}}(t,r_\ast(t))
  \cong \R^{d(t)}.
  \label{eq:topological-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
The open ball is important: an open $d$-ball is homeomorphic to $\R^d$,
whereas a closed ball is compact. In a stratified space,
Equation~\eqref{eq:topological-hypothesis} is expected only on regular pieces;
points near boundaries or intersections may have different local structure.

A homeomorphism preserves topology but not Euclidean distances, volumes, or
probability density. Thus Equation~\eqref{eq:topological-hypothesis} alone does
not imply a PMF for observed radii. To obtain a testable statement, we add a
local measure assumption:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal H_{0,t}(d):\qquad
  X\mid\{X\in\mathcal E,\ \|X-t\|_2<r_\ast(t)\}
  \text{ is uniform with respect to local $d$-dimensional volume.}
  \label{eq:localized-null}
\end{equation}
This is the tangent-ball approximation: at scale $r_\ast(t)$, the support is
approximately Euclidean and the sampling density is approximately constant.
It is deliberately stronger than the topological hypothesis. Therefore,
rejection of Equation~\eqref{eq:localized-null} is evidence against local
homogeneous volume at the chosen scale, not a logical disproof of local
homeomorphism.

\section{The Concentric-Shell PMF}

Write $R=\|X-t\|_2$. The volume of a $d$-dimensional ball of radius $r$ is
$c_d r^d$. Under Equation~\eqref{eq:localized-null}, for $0\le r\le r_\ast$,
\begin{align}
  F_d(r)
  &=\Prb(R\le r\mid R\le r_\ast,\mathcal H_{0,t}(d)) \\
  &=\frac{\vol(\Ball_d(t,r))}{\vol(\Ball_d(t,r_\ast))}
    =\left(\frac{r}{r_\ast}\right)^d.
  \label{eq:radial-cdf}
\end{align}
The radial density is consequently
\begin{equation}
  f_d(r)=\frac{d}{r_\ast^d}r^{d-1},\qquad 0<r<r_\ast.
  \label{eq:radial-density}
\end{equation}
This density is not constant in radius. A uniform point in a high-dimensional
ball is usually near the boundary because outer shells contain more volume.

Choose concentric boundaries
\[
  0=r_0<r_1<\cdots<r_K=r_\ast
\]
and let $H_i=k$ when the $i$th neighbor radius lies in
$(r_{k-1},r_k]$. The null PMF is
\begin{align}
  p_k(d)
  &=\Prb(H_i=k\mid\mathcal H_{0,t}(d)) \\
  &=F_d(r_k)-F_d(r_{k-1})
    =\frac{r_k^d-r_{k-1}^d}{r_\ast^d},
  \qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
  \label{eq:shell-pmf}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:shell-pmf} is the requested shell PMF. It is simply the
fraction of total intrinsic volume lying between two consecutive radii.

For easy interpretation, choose equal-volume boundaries
\begin{equation}
  r_k=r_\ast\left(\frac{k}{K}\right)^{1/d}.
  \label{eq:equal-volume-boundaries}
\end{equation}
Then every shell has the same null probability:
\begin{equation}
  p_k(d)=\frac{1}{K}.
  \label{eq:uniform-shell-pmf}
\end{equation}
The shells are generally not equal in radial width. In high dimension the
inner shells must be wider because a small radius contains little volume.

\subsection{Why the count vector is multinomial}

Suppose first that $t$, $r_\ast$, $d$, and the shell boundaries are fixed, and
that $X_1,\ldots,X_N$ are conditionally independent draws from the local null.
Each shell label $H_i$ is then a categorical random variable with
\[
  \Prb(H_i=k)=p_k(d),\qquad k=1,\ldots,K.
\]
Define the shell counts
\begin{equation}
  n_k=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf 1\{H_i=k\},
  \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{K}n_k=N.
  \label{eq:shell-counts}
\end{equation}
For one particular assignment of labels having these counts, independence
gives probability $\prod_k p_k^{n_k}$. There are
$N!/\prod_k n_k!$ assignments with the same count vector. Summing their equal
probabilities yields
\begin{equation}
  \Prb(n_1,\ldots,n_K\mid\mathcal H_{0,t})
  =\frac{N!}{\prod_{k=1}^{K}n_k!}\prod_{k=1}^{K}p_k^{n_k}.
  \label{eq:multinomial-pmf}
\end{equation}
Thus $(n_1,\ldots,n_K)\sim\operatorname{Multinomial}(N,P_d)$. The result is not
an extra modeling choice: it follows from independent categorical shell
membership.

In our nearest-neighbor implementation, the farthest of $M$ retrieved
neighbors defines $r_\ast$, and the remaining $N=M-1$ interior radii are tested.
Under an ideal continuous local-volume model, conditioning on the outer order
statistic produces the same truncated radial law for the unordered interior
points. Fitting $d$ from those points makes the final shell labels dependent,
however, so the raw multinomial reference is no longer exact. Section~4
handles this dependence by reproducing the fitting step during calibration.

\section{Fitting Dimension and Constructing the Test}

\subsection{A scale-free radial coordinate}

Equation~\eqref{eq:radial-cdf} implies that
\begin{equation}
  U_i=\left(\frac{R_i}{r_\ast}\right)^d
  \sim\operatorname{Uniform}(0,1).
  \label{eq:volume-coordinate}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, set
\begin{equation}
  Z_i=\log\frac{r_\ast}{R_i}.
  \label{eq:log-radius}
\end{equation}
For $z\ge0$,
\begin{align}
  \Prb(Z_i>z)
  &=\Prb(R_i<r_\ast e^{-z})
    =\left(e^{-z}\right)^d=e^{-dz},
\end{align}
so $Z_i\sim\operatorname{Exp}(d)$. Its likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  L(d;Z_1,\ldots,Z_N)=d^N\exp\left(-d\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_i\right),
\end{equation}
and differentiation of the log likelihood gives
\begin{equation}
  \widehat d=\frac{N}{\sum_i Z_i}=\frac{1}{\overline Z}.
  \label{eq:dimension-mle}
\end{equation}
We then form the fitted volume coordinates
\begin{equation}
  \widehat U_i
  =\left(\frac{R_i}{r_\ast}\right)^{\widehat d}
  =\exp\left(-\frac{Z_i}{\overline Z}\right)
  \label{eq:fitted-volume-coordinate}
\end{equation}
and count them in the $K$ intervals
$((k-1)/K,k/K]$. Under a known $d$, these are exactly equal-probability shells.
With $\widehat d$, they remain the natural fitted shells but require calibrated
inference.

\subsection{Likelihood-ratio statistic}

Let $q_k=n_k/N$. Under the null PMF $P=(p_1,\ldots,p_K)$, the multinomial
likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  L(P;n)=\frac{N!}{\prod_k n_k!}\prod_{k=1}^{K}p_k^{n_k}.
  \label{eq:null-likelihood}
\end{equation}
The unrestricted, or saturated, multinomial model is maximized at
$\widehat q_k=n_k/N$, giving
\begin{equation}
  L(\widehat Q;n)=\frac{N!}{\prod_k n_k!}\prod_{k=1}^{K}q_k^{n_k}.
  \label{eq:saturated-likelihood}
\end{equation}
The generalized likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda
  =\frac{L(P;n)}{L(\widehat Q;n)}
  =\prod_{k:q_k>0}\left(\frac{p_k}{q_k}\right)^{n_k}.
\end{equation}
Taking negative twice the log gives the multinomial deviance
\begin{align}
  T_N(t)
  &=-2\log\Lambda \\
  &=2\sum_{k:q_k>0}n_k\log\frac{q_k}{p_k} \\
  &=2N\sum_{k:q_k>0}q_k\log\frac{q_k}{p_k}
    =2N\KL(\widehat Q\|P).
  \label{eq:lrt-statistic}
\end{align}
We use the convention $0\log0=0$. For the fitted equal-volume shells,
$p_k=1/K$, so the implemented statistic is especially simple:
\begin{equation}
  T_N(t)=2\sum_{k:n_k>0}n_k\log\frac{n_k}{N/K}.
  \label{eq:equal-shell-lrt}
\end{equation}

The statistic has an intuitive interpretation. It is zero when every shell has
its expected count. It increases when mass accumulates in too few shells, and
each term records the contribution of one shell. This makes a count-residual
plot a direct visual explanation of a rejection.

\subsection{Why the factor two appears}

The factor two is the standard likelihood-ratio normalization. Write
$q_k=p_k+\delta_k$, where $\sum_k\delta_k=0$. A second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \KL(Q\|P)
  &=\sum_k(p_k+\delta_k)\log\left(1+\frac{\delta_k}{p_k}\right) \\
  &=\frac{1}{2}\sum_k\frac{\delta_k^2}{p_k}+o(\|\delta\|^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
  2N\KL(Q\|P)
  \approx N\sum_k\frac{(q_k-p_k)^2}{p_k}
  =\sum_k\frac{(n_k-Np_k)^2}{Np_k},
  \label{eq:pearson-equivalence}
\end{equation}
which is Pearson's chi-square statistic. The two cancels the $1/2$ in the
quadratic expansion, yielding the conventional chi-square scale. For fixed
$P$ and regular large-sample conditions, $T_N\Rightarrow\chi^2_{K-1}$.

This asymptotic reference is useful for intuition, but it is not our decision
threshold. Fitted dimension, data-dependent shells, moderate neighborhood
sizes, and occasional sparse counts can change the finite-sample law.

\subsection{Monte Carlo calibration of the complete fitted procedure}

Under the null, $Z_i\sim\operatorname{Exp}(d)$. Because
$Z_i/\overline Z$ is unchanged by multiplying every $Z_i$ by the same positive
constant, the statistic is independent of the unknown rate $d$. We may
therefore calibrate with unit-rate exponential variables:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Draw $Z_1^{(b)},\ldots,Z_N^{(b)}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
  \operatorname{Exp}(1)$ for $b=1,\ldots,B$.
  \item Refit the scale in each replicate and compute
  $\widehat U_i^{(b)}=\exp(-Z_i^{(b)}/\overline Z^{(b)})$.
  \item Count the fitted coordinates in the same $K$ equal-width intervals and
  compute $T_N^{(b)}$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:equal-shell-lrt}.
  \item Let $c_\alpha$ be the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of
  $T_N^{(1)},\ldots,T_N^{(B)}$. Reject when $T_N(t)>c_\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}
The finite-simulation upper-tail p-value is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat p(t)=
  \frac{1+\sum_{b=1}^{B}\mathbf 1\{T_N^{(b)}\ge T_N(t)\}}{B+1}.
  \label{eq:mc-pvalue}
\end{equation}
The plus-one correction prevents a reported p-value of zero and gives a valid
randomization-style finite-simulation p-value. Every reported experiment uses
$K=8$, $\alpha=0.05$, and $B=50{,}000$ calibration replicates.

\subsection{Why this statistic matches the scientific question}

The localized hypothesis is explicitly a claim about the shell PMF, so a
multinomial likelihood ratio tests the proposed object directly. It also has
four practical advantages: it is scale-free after fitting $d$; it summarizes
departure in the KL geometry already used by maximum likelihood; it remains
well defined when a shell is empty; and its shellwise terms can be visualized.
A maximum single-shell discrepancy would ignore distributed departures, while
an uncalibrated chi-square approximation would not reproduce our fitted-shell
pipeline. The likelihood-ratio statistic plus full-pipeline calibration is the
simplest procedure that preserves both interpretability and finite-sample
control for the analysis we actually run.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

For every anchor, we retrieve $M$ nearest neighbors, set $r_\ast$ to the
distance of the farthest retrieved neighbor, and test the remaining $N=M-1$
interior distances. We estimate $\widehat d$ from Equation~\eqref{eq:dimension-mle},
form $K=8$ fitted equal-mass shells, compute $T_N$, and compare it with the
Monte Carlo critical value for that $N$. A displayed ``deviance ratio'' means
$T_N/c_{0.05}$, so values above one reject.

Anchor neighborhoods overlap, so anchor-level decisions are not independent.
We report the raw rejection fraction as a geometric diagnostic, not as a
familywise-error-controlled global hypothesis test. Cross-pipeline rates should
also be interpreted descriptively because the representations, ambient
dimensions, normalizations, and neighborhood sizes differ.

\section{Synthetic Size and Power}

$U\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1.60,0.45)$ for excess mass near the outer radius.

\section{ImageNet Patch Experiment}

We use 16 ImageNet validation images, center-crop each image to $224\times224$,
split it into a $14\times14$ grid of $16\times16$ patches, and represent each
patch by a standardized $4\times4$ RGB thumbnail. This deliberately transparent
feature map makes the input geometry visible. The pool contains $3{,}136$
patches. For every patch anchor, $M=96$ nearest patches define $N=95$ tested
interior radii.

The calibrated critical value is $12.859$. We reject at $1{,}036$ of $3{,}136$
anchors, or $33.04\%$. The median fitted dimension is $5.75$, and the median
deviance ratio is $0.715$. Rejections are spatially heterogeneous: some objects
and backgrounds contain many visually repeated patches, while distinctive
boundaries or rare textures produce sharply unbalanced shell counts.

\section{LlamaGen: VQ Tokenizer with Autoregressive Generation}

LlamaGen combines a VQ tokenizer with raster-order autoregressive generation
\cite{sun2024llamagen}. We analyze all $16{,}384$ normalized codebook vectors.
For each code, $M=128$ nearest codes define $N=127$ tested radii. The calibrated
critical value is $13.004$.

The shell test rejects $14{,}226$ codes, or $86.83\%$. The median fitted
dimension is $6.52$ and the median deviance ratio is $1.72$. Codes previously
identified as belonging to large decoder fibers reject at $88.55\%$, compared
with $86.30\%$ for the rest. Thus mismatch is widespread throughout this
codebook and only modestly enriched in the large-fiber subset.

Joining code-level decisions to 64 ImageNet token sequences yields a target-code
rejection fraction of $86.68\%$ over $16{,}384$ positions. At the position
level, rejected target codes have higher branch entropy and lower branch KS
than nonrejected targets. These comparisons are exploratory because positions
within an image are dependent and rejection is so common that the comparison
groups are highly imbalanced. They show an association in this pipeline, not a
causal effect of codebook geometry on generation.

\section{VAR: VQ Tokenizer with Next-Scale Generation}

VAR predicts progressively finer token maps instead of using raster order
\cite{tian2024var}. The released d16 and d30 generators share a $4{,}096$-entry,
32-dimensional VQ codebook. We L2-normalize the code vectors and test $N=127$
interior radii per code. The calibrated critical value is $12.927$.

The test rejects $544$ of $4{,}096$ codes, or $13.28\%$, with median fitted
dimension $28.83$ and median deviance ratio $0.551$. The rejection rate is much
lower than for LlamaGen, illustrating that local radial geometry is strongly
tokenizer-dependent even when both systems use discrete visual tokens and
autoregressive generation.

To test whether rejected target codes coincide with generator uncertainty, we
compare rejected and nonrejected token positions within each image, then apply
an exact one-sided sign-flip test to the 16 image-level differences. Table~
\ref{tab:var-generator} reports p-values for ImageNet-conditioned positions.
No metric is significant for either model depth. The same analysis on generated
samples also yields no p-value below $0.50$. Thus VAR supplies a useful negative
control: codebook mismatch does not automatically imply unstable next-token
prediction.

The common result is not that every vision representation fails the local
volume law. Rather, the degree and spatial pattern of failure depend strongly
on the representation. ImageNet patch space is mixed, LlamaGen's normalized
codebook departs broadly, and the VAR codebook is much closer to the fitted
equal-shell model. This is the empirical story supported by the test.

These findings are compatible with a stratified-manifold picture. Boundaries,
intersections, and transitions between local modes should create neighborhoods
whose radial mass cannot be summarized by one smooth dimension. However, the
same rejection can be caused by nonuniform density on a single smooth
manifold, anisotropy, curvature at the selected scale, an inappropriate metric,
or finite-codebook quantization. A radial test cannot distinguish these causes
on its own.

The finite VQ codebooks require particular care. A finite set is not literally
a positive-dimensional manifold. Here the codebook is used as a geometric
proxy for the latent regions represented by its codes. The test measures how
the discrete prototypes occupy embedding space, not the topology of the
underlying continuous data manifold.

The generator experiments add a second lesson. A tokenizer can have strongly
nonuniform local geometry without a correspondingly strong or universal change
in generator uncertainty. LlamaGen shows position-level associations, whereas
VAR does not show reliable image-level effects. Tokenizer geometry and
generator behavior are related empirical layers, not interchangeable claims.

\section{Limitations and Stronger Evidence for Stratification}

The localized null is intentionally simple, and its rejection is
non-identifying. A stronger empirical case for stratification would combine:
\begin{itemize}
  \item shell-test rejections that persist over a range of neighborhood sizes;
  \item stable local dimension estimates within putative regular regions;
  \item tangent-space and angular diagnostics that separate anisotropy from
  radial density variation;
  \item boundary or intersection evidence that aligns with changes in local
  dimension or decoder behavior;
  \item direct topological summaries, such as persistent local homology, on
  sufficiently dense continuous features; and
  \item held-out replication across tokenizers, metrics, and datasets.
\end{itemize}

The present rejection fractions are not corrected for simultaneous testing,
and overlapping neighborhoods induce strong dependence. They should be read as
descriptive maps of where the local-volume surrogate is plausible. Formal
claims about the proportion of nonnull anchors would require a separate
multiple-testing analysis with dependence-aware uncertainty.

\section{Summary}

The global hypothesis proposes a locally Euclidean organization of vision
representations. To make a finite-sample test possible, we add local metric and
measure assumptions. Uniform intrinsic volume implies
\[
  \Prb(R\le r\mid R\le r_\ast)=\left(\frac{r}{r_\ast}\right)^d
\]
and the concentric-shell PMF
\[
  p_k(d)=\frac{r_k^d-r_{k-1}^d}{r_\ast^d}.
\]
Equal-volume boundaries make this PMF uniform. Independent shell memberships
then yield a multinomial count vector, whose likelihood ratio against the
saturated model is
\[
  T_N(t)=2N\KL(\widehat Q\|P).
\]
We fit dimension through exponential log-radii, repeat that fit inside every
Monte Carlo replicate, and reject when the observed deviance exceeds the
calibrated critical value.

The synthetic experiment verifies size and power. The real-data experiments
show substantial but architecture-dependent departures: $33.04\%$ for
ImageNet patches, $86.83\%$ for LlamaGen codes, and $13.28\%$ for VAR codes.
The defensible conclusion is that vision representations have heterogeneous
local measure geometry. This supports continued investigation of stratified
structure, but topology requires additional evidence beyond radial shell
counts.
